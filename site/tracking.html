How to read this Every game gets two separate picks: a moneyline pick (who wins the game outright) and a spread pick (which side beats the betting line). They often disagree, so they are tracked separately. "Units" is profit from betting one unit on every pick at the prices the sportsbook actually posted — positive means the picks made money, negative means they lost. Spread picks need about 52.4% to break even; green is beating that, red is losing money.

Closing line value

What closing line value means The most reliable test of whether picks carry an edge. If a pick is genuinely good, the market tends to move toward that side before kickoff — the number taken beats the number at close. Above 50% means the picks are on the right side of where money goes; below 50% means the opposite, and no run of wins changes that verdict. Win rate over one season is mostly noise; this is not. Opening prices are published for college games only, so the NFL cannot be measured this way from free data.

Picks vs Leans

Picks, Leans and Pixel’s Picks explained A Pick is a side where the calibrated probability beats the price — the model thinks the bet is worth making. A Lean is everything else: the model has an opinion and shows all its reasoning, but the price does not justify backing it. Leans are information, not bets. Pixel’s Picks are the single highest-value play each week.
